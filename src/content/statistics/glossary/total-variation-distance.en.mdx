## What it says

For two probability distributions $P$ and $Q$ on the same set, the total variation distance runs over every event $A$ and takes the largest gap:

$$
\|P - Q\| = \max_{A} |P(A) - Q(A)| = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{x} |P(x) - Q(x)|
$$

<Cite id="aldousDiaconis1986">The two forms agree, and the maximum is attained at $\{x : P(x) > Q(x)\}$</Cite>. The distance always lies between 0 and 1.

There is a working reading of this definition. Think of an event $A$ as a test: I look at the outcome and declare it came from $P$. The distance is the edge available to the best such test. A distance of 0.334 means no test anyone can design beats guessing by more than 33 percent; a distance of 0.01 means nothing tells them apart.

## Where it comes from

It is the standard way of measuring how far apart two distributions are, and in shuffling and Markov chain problems it defines what "random by now" means. <Cite id="aldousDiaconis1986">Aldous and Diaconis wrote it in 1986 as $d(k) = \|Q^{k*} - U\|$ to ask how far a deck is from uniform after $k$ shuffles</Cite>.

## An example that happened

Three examples, running from obvious to nearly invisible.

**A glimpsed bottom card: 0.98.** <Cite id="aldousDiaconis1986">Take a well-shuffled deck of $n$ cards and suppose you catch sight of the bottom card. Your distribution is uniform over the permutations that put that card at the bottom, and it sits $1 - 1/n$ away from uniform</Cite>. For 52 cards that is 0.98, even though the other 51 really are random: one event, "the bottom card is that one", moves from probability $1/52$ to 1.

**A deck shuffled four times: 1.000.** <Cite id="bayerDiaconis1992">$k$ shuffles leave at most $2^k$ rising sequences, so four leave at most 16, while a uniformly random 52-card deck averages 26.5 and falls to 16 or below with probability $4.67 \times 10^{-7}$</Cite>. Take "at most 16 rising sequences" as the event and the two probabilities are 1 and roughly four in ten million, so the distance sits at 1. The test here is nothing more than counting.

**A deck shuffled seven times: 0.334.** Set it beside a randomly arranged deck and ask someone to point out the shuffled one: guessing wins half the time, the best available check $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \times 0.334 = 66.7\%$. <Cite id="bayerDiaconis1992">By the tenth shuffle the distance is 0.043</Cite>, which leaves 52.1 percent — close to a coin flip.

## How it is misread

**Reading it as an average gap.** It is a maximum. In the example above almost every event has barely moved, and the distance is still near 1. <Cite id="aldousDiaconis1986">The paper calls the distance "unforgiving" of small deviations</Cite>.

**Reading a small distance as sameness.** A distance of 0.043 says no test gets an edge above 4.3 percent. It does not say the distributions match.

**Comparing it against a different distance.** Separation, $l_\infty$ and information each have their own definition and their numbers do not line up; <Cite id="diaconisFulmanHolmes2013">for a given distribution the total variation distance never exceeds the separation distance</Cite>. Change the distance and the answer to "how many times" changes with it.
